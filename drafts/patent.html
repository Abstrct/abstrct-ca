Method and System for Placing a Game Inside a Thing That Is Not a Game

Prior art:
None found. The examiner looked briefly, then started playing.

Abstract

A method by which an ordinary system, such as a database, a cryptocurrency, a blockchain or a 16-bit games console, is made to contain a game it was not designed to contain, after which strangers are invited to play it, break it, or both.

Background

[0001]Before this invention, databases contained data.

[0002]A need therefore existed for a database that contained a space war. See Schemaverse, first demonstrated at DEF CON 19 (2011), where 108 players ran over a million queries in four days.

Claims

What is claimed is:

  1. A method, comprising: (a) obtaining a database; (b) putting a space war in it; and (c) inviting the audience at a hacker conference to attack it.
  2. The method of claim 1, wherein the database is instead a cryptocurrency, and the war is fought by robots shooting money at each other (“Coindroids,” DEF CON 22–28).
  3. The method of claim 2, wherein the cryptocurrency is instead an entire blockchain, and some of the players are AI agents (“Structs”).
  4. The method of claim 3, performed again in a different outfit, wherein the outfit is selected from the group consisting of a toy box (pippop), a sky of dragons (wyrmhodl), a Dreamcast game (structs.xyz) and fur beasts (Furrency).
  5. The method of claim 1, wherein the database is instead a Super Nintendo, and the space war is instead seven events across Toronto, including TTC Surfing (“Toronto Games”).
  6. The method of any preceding claim, carried out quickly, after the conference has already begun (see Tokens.fyi; Key Ceremony Guide).
  7. A system for keeping everyone else honest, comprising a non-profit (C4, co-founded 2014) and a security standard the inventor helped write.
  8. The method of claim 1, accompanied by a DJ set.
